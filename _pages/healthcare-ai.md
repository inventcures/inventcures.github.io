  <li class="pd-item">
    <h3><a href="https://x.com/_jasonwei/status/2112259970108686505" target="_blank" rel="noopener">AI will not automate radiologists like you think</a></h3>
    <p class="pd-meta">Jason Wei (<a href="https://x.com/_jasonwei" target="_blank" rel="noopener">@_jasonwei</a>) · X · <time datetime="2026-10-20">20 Oct 2026</time></p>
    <p class="pd-summary">Ten years after Hinton said to stop training radiologists, demand for them is higher than ever. Wei argues it stays that way: AI's jagged edge makes it a triage tool first across radiology's thousands of conditions, cheaper reads create elastic demand (Jevons paradox) for many more scans, and medicine keeps inventing new things to read.</p>

  <li class="pd-item">
    <h3><a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/08/technology/ai-chatbots-urgent-care.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Clinical Trial Indicates A.I. Chatbots Could Aid Urgent Care</a></h3>
    <p class="pd-meta">Cade Metz · The New York Times · <time datetime="2026-10-08">8 Oct 2026</time></p>
    <p class="pd-summary">Nearly 100 urgent care patients at Beth Israel Deaconess talked with Google's AMIE before seeing a physician. There were no safety stops, AMIE's differential held the final diagnosis 90% of the time, and about 75% of clinicians found its output helpful (<a href="https://research.google/pubs/a-prospective-clinical-feasibility-study-of-a-conversational-diagnostic-ai-in-an-ambulatory-primary-care-clinic/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">study in The Lancet</a>).</p>
    <p class="pd-summary"><strong>Spencer Dorn's take:</strong> AI is moving from writing notes about the doctor-patient conversation to running parts of it, much as a teaching attending supervises several trainees at once. We shouldn't romanticize the status quo, but we must be deliberate about what we give up (<a href="https://www.linkedin.com/posts/spencerdorn_my-take-on-the-direction-suggested-by-the-share-7515908032132751361-FaUi/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">LinkedIn post</a>, 13 Oct 2026).</p>
    <p class="pd-thread"><a href="https://www.linkedin.com/posts/spencerdorn_my-take-on-the-direction-suggested-by-the-share-7515908032132751361-FaUi/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Dorn's post on LinkedIn ↗</a> · <a href="/files/spencer-dorn-amie-lancet.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Full text (PDF) ↗</a></p>

  <li class="pd-item">
    <h3><a href="https://robertwachter.substack.com/p/on-straw-men-and-doormen" target="_blank" rel="noopener">On Straw Men and Doormen</a></h3>
    <p class="pd-meta">Robert Wachter · Substack · <time datetime="2026-08-27">27 Aug 2026</time></p>
    <p class="pd-summary">A gentle rebuttal to Khosla and Emanuel's JAMA argument that a physician in the loop degrades AI diagnosis. Wachter grants that AI often wins on curated facts, but points to garbage-in patient data and the "doorman fallacy" (most clinical work isn't diagnosis), and predicts AI-first primary care alongside AI-enabled clinicians: "And, Not Or."</p>
  </li>

    <h3><a href="https://sites.prh.com/agiantleapbook" target="_blank" rel="noopener">A Giant Leap: How AI Is Transforming Healthcare and What That Means for Our Future</a></h3>
    <p class="pd-meta">Robert Wachter · Book · <time datetime="2026-02-03">3 Feb 2026</time></p>
    <p class="pd-summary">Bob Wachter's book on how AI is reshaping care delivery, the clinician's role and the health system, with a special section on medical education.</p>

  <li class="pd-item">
    <h3><a href="https://robertwachter.substack.com/p/will-ai-rescue-primary-care" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Will AI Rescue Primary Care? Eventually, But Not Yet</a></h3>
    <p class="pd-meta">Robert Wachter · Substack · <time datetime="2025-10-08">8 Oct 2025</time></p>
    <p class="pd-summary">Starting from the collapse of Forward, the AI-heavy primary care startup, Wachter argues that primary care is in crisis and concierge care is too expensive for most people. He predicts a tiered system: AI-first care with clinician oversight for basic plans, human-first care for those with more resources or complex needs. He compares it to economy and first-class seats on a plane.</p>
    <p class="pd-note">The airline analogy is the heart of it. Economy, business and first class differ in legroom, meals and how much a human attends to you, but every seat flies under the same high minimum safety floor. AI-first care for the "economy" tier is acceptable only if that floor holds: judged against the care people can actually get (often very little, in much of India), and with a safe, well-routed handoff to a human doctor when the case needs one. The analogy stretches further. Autopilot flies most of the trip, yet pilots still go back to the simulator to practise rare failures. Doctors trained alongside agentic AI should likewise keep the skills to give safe care on the day the AI goes down.</p>
  </li>

  <li class="pd-item">
    <h3><a href="https://worksinprogress.co/issue/the-algorithm-will-see-you-now/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">The algorithm will see you now</a></h3>
    <p class="pd-meta">Deena Mousa · Works in Progress, Issue 20 · <time datetime="2025-09-25">25 Sep 2025</time></p>
    <p class="pd-summary">Radiology looked like the easiest specialty for AI to automate, yet radiologists are busier than ever. Models that beat benchmarks stumble on real hospital data, regulation and malpractice insurance slow adoption, and diagnosis is only part of the job; many knowledge jobs may follow the same path.</p>

## Medical Education

<ul class="pd-list">

  <li class="pd-item">
    <h3><a href="https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/article-abstract/2855358" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Physician Training for the AI Era: Rethinking a Century-Old Wager</a></h3>
    <p class="pd-meta">Jesse Burk-Rafel &amp; Robert M. Wachter · JAMA Viewpoint · <time datetime="2026-10-14">14 Oct 2026</time></p>
    <p class="pd-summary">Medical training has long bet that mastering knowledge plus supervised experience yields clinical judgment. As AI takes over cognitive work, learners risk "never-skilling", so the authors propose strengthening the skills AI makes more valuable, building the ones it makes newly necessary, and de-emphasizing the rest.</p>
