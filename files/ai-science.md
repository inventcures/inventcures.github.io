# AI × Science

I want a future in which AI agents help people ask better questions, explore more possibilities and turn reliable discoveries into benefits that reach more people. Science and mathematics offer extraordinary opportunities for this kind of collaboration, especially in medicine and biology, where better understanding can ultimately mean less suffering and more healthy life. I am interested in human–AI teams and collaborations across laboratories around the world, with each contributing strengths the others lack.

Progress should deepen human agency and meaning: the freedom to choose what matters, understand the work, challenge an answer and share in discovery. That means evaluating evidence rather than just fluent output, preserving uncertainty and giving people real authority over consequential decisions. It also means designing against accidental harm and deliberate misuse, taking dual-use risks seriously, and making the benefits of scientific capability broadly accessible.

### Google Research: building blocks for autonomous wet labs

One blog post and three papers released together. Start with the overview, then read how the lab is made checkable (VERID), how a fast loop exposes agent shortcuts (aptamers), and how a human–agent split cuts design cost (epitope excision).

#### Grounding AI agents in biological reality: Building blocks for autonomous wet labs

Pooja Rao, Petar Sirkovic and Annalisa Pawlosky argue that agents should run wet-lab experiments, learn from the results and improve, with humans directing. Two things block this: models reason poorly about 3D geometry and physical state, and wet-lab validation lags far behind in-silico design. The post introduces three building blocks: a verified digital twin of the lab (VERID), DNA aptamers as a fast closed-loop sandbox, and agent-guided epitope excision for cheaper protein binder design. Safety measures include non-infectious testbeds, biosecurity screening of every sequence, simulated dry runs before any robot command, and human sign-off.

- Type: Blog post
- Published: 16 Oct 2026
- Resource: [Grounding AI agents in biological reality: Building blocks for autonomous wet labs](https://research.google/blog/grounding-ai-agents-in-biological-reality-building-blocks-for-autonomous-wet-labs/)
- OP: not yet verified
- Topics: Biology, Agents, Biopharma, Human–AI collaboration, Verification
- Margin note: The theme is grounding: agents may not act on the world until a simulator, a sequencer or a binding assay can say whether they were right. Full autonomy is called an open problem; humans set strategy and authorize runs.

#### VERID: VERified Infrastructure for Discovery Laboratories

A semantic digital twin of a robotic workcell, built on PyLabRobot, that tracks arm geometry, well volumes, reagents and tip inventory as a live 3D state tree. Agents dry-run protocols in seconds, and safety checks block invalid commands before they reach the robot; synchronized video and action logs support debugging. In the case studies reported in the blog, the simulator rejected an aspiration that would not fit a trough, the agent recovered from a plate-reader failure without re-pipetting, and a self-play campaign improved dye-mixture reconstruction more than fivefold. Summary from the Google blog; the PDF has not yet been read here.

- Type: Paper
- Published: 16 Oct 2026
- Resource: [VERID: VERified Infrastructure for Discovery Laboratories](https://www.gstatic.com/science-ai/verid_semantic_lab.pdf)
- OP: not yet verified
- Topics: Biology, Agents, Verification, Infrastructure
- Margin note: A digital twin is a verifier for physical actions: it can say “this will collide” or “that well is empty” before reagents are spent. It turns part of lab work from judgement into checking.

#### Aptamers as a sandbox for lab-in-the-loop agentic molecular discovery

DNA aptamers can be synthesized in pools and read out by sequencing, so a design–test–analyze cycle takes about 7–10 days. Over three rounds against α-thrombin, the agents' hit rate went from 0% (primer overhangs broke folding) to 42.1% at 98.5% specificity. Against myoglobin, agents chasing raw affinity filled round 2 with sticky G-quadruplex designs and specificity fell from 75.0% to 19.1%; they spotted the artifact, capped those motifs and recovered to 72.4% specificity with a 33.5% on-target hit rate. Figures as reported in the Google blog; the PDF has not yet been read here.

- Type: Paper
- Published: 16 Oct 2026
- Resource: [Aptamers as a sandbox for lab-in-the-loop agentic molecular discovery](https://www.gstatic.com/science-ai/agentic_aptamer_sandbox.pdf)
- OP: not yet verified
- Topics: Biology, Agents, Biopharma, Verification
- Margin note: Myoglobin round 2 is Goodhart in a test tube: told to maximize binding, the agents found molecules that stick to everything. The wet lab caught it, because specificity was measured alongside affinity.

#### Agent-guided epitope excision enables economical de novo binder design

Designing binders to FAP, a large receptor overexpressed in solid tumors, the team re-folds only the local binding pocket instead of the whole target, speeding structure prediction 8.6x without losing accuracy. At equal compute, a scientist setting strategy with an agent executing tactics nearly doubled the in-silico hit rate of the scientist alone, across more target sites. Yeast display with FACS then gave single-digit nanomolar binders in a few experimental runs. Figures as reported in the Google blog; the PDF has not yet been read here.

- Type: Paper
- Published: 16 Oct 2026
- Resource: [Agent-guided epitope excision enables economical de novo binder design](https://www.gstatic.com/science-ai/epitope_excision.pdf)
- OP: not yet verified
- Topics: Biology, Agents, Biopharma, Human–AI collaboration
- Margin note: Folding cost grows with the square of target length, so trimming a target to a pocket one-seventh its size cuts that term about 49-fold (the reported end-to-end speedup is 8.6x). The human sets strategy and the agent executes tactics: a division of labor, not full autonomy.

#### Benchmarking biopharma knowledge work

Convoke benchmarks models on six biopharma work products outside R&D: target safety assessment, tumor landscape, shelved assets, catalyst calendar, clinical benchmark and commercial forecast. Domain experts wrote penalty-based rubrics (major, moderate, minor errors), and an LLM grader with research tools scored each criterion against a citation. They report that frontier OpenAI models lead and that models handle structured spreadsheet compilation well but struggle with narrative synthesis and judgment-heavy forecasts. Frontier models cost more than 3x as much per work product. The grader has not yet been validated against expert grading.

### A thread: forgetting to escape idea basins

Two papers ten days apart reach the same answer: long-running research agents get stuck refining their own past ideas, and the cure is to forget. Read Fork-and-Flush first, then the more aggressive version.

#### Fork-and-Flush: Escaping Idea Basins in Autoresearch Agents

Cai, Papailiopoulos and colleagues find that independent runs of the same autoresearch agent on the same task end at very different scores, and that extra compute does not close the gap. Comparing solutions by what they do suggests each run stays inside a local “idea basin”. Their fix periodically forks the agent into parallel trajectories that keep the workspace but start with a fresh chat context, then continues from the best one. Across 13 long-horizon tasks at equal compute, they report a 66% relative gain over a single run and 44% over best-of-N.

- Type: Preprint
- Published: 05 Oct 2026
- Resource: [Fork-and-Flush: Escaping Idea Basins in Autoresearch Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07447)
- OP: not yet verified
- Topics: Agents, Scientific discovery, Research taste
- Margin note: Think of a hiker who only ever steps uphill from where they stand: they reach the top of the nearest hill, not the highest one. A fresh context is a new starting point on the map.

#### Scaling Discovery Requires Forgetting

Tan, Dimakis, Zaharia and colleagues at Berkeley show that running an autoresearch agent longer, running agents in parallel, or letting them talk all plateau, because agents anchor on their growing history. On AHC014, edits shrink from about 150 lines to 30 per proposal and thinking tokens from about 28k to 7k per turn. Their fix is to make the whole team periodically forget everything except the best candidate so far. Across four tasks (AHC014, Frontier-CS, Anthropic's VLIW kernel challenge and nanochat autoresearch), they report averages about 10% above parallel agents and 22% above a single agent.

- Type: Preprint
- Published: 14 Oct 2026
- Resource: [Scaling Discovery Requires Forgetting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20563)
- OP: [Shangyin Tan on X](https://x.com/ShangyinT/status/2110771398209863821)
- Topics: Agents, Scientific discovery, Research taste
- Margin note: Fork-and-Flush keeps the workspace and clears the chat. This paper clears both and keeps only the best answer. Shrinking edit sizes are a measurable symptom of an agent getting stuck.
- Alex Dimakis on X: https://x.com/AlexGDimakis/status/2110808302796108249

### AgentRun: a harness for repetitive knowledge work

Miguel Rios (Grep.ai) describes a harness in which a frontier agent first does a job from a written procedure, keeps notes and traces, then writes a typed workflow and “fires itself”. The workflow mixes agent steps, plain code and typed questions answered by a small calibrated model (Jev), under the rule “Jev decides what a thing is; code decides what follows.” A verify clause checks each claim against the evidence the agent cited. On an anti-money-laundering alert review, the author reports cost per alert falling from $2.89 to $0.25 and judged accuracy rising from 90% to 95%, scored on a held-out set they never looked at.

- Type: Article
- Published: 18 Sep 2026

- Margin note: A practical answer to the taste question. Closed decisions (“is this a match?”) become typed questions that a cheap calibrated model answers and code routes. Open judgment stays with the agent or a human. A held-out set guards against grading your own homework.
