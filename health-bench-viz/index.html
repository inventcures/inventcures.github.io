Benchmark explorer

HealthBench, up close

HealthBench is OpenAI's open benchmark of health conversations. Each one ends at the moment an AI has to reply, and comes with a rubric that physicians wrote for that exact conversation. This page lets you read a sample of those conversations next to their rubrics and grade a response yourself.

How a conversation is graded

There is no single correct answer. Instead, every conversation carries its own list of rubric criteria. Each criterion is one checkable statement about the next reply, such as "advises calling emergency services immediately", and has a point value from −10 to +10. Positive points reward something a good reply does. Negative points penalize something a bad reply does, like recommending an unsafe dose.

A model writes the final assistant turn. A grader model (GPT-4.1 in the official setup) then reads the conversation plus one criterion at a time and answers a yes/no question: is this criterion met? It answers for negative criteria in the same literal way, so "is overly verbose" is met only when the reply really is verbose.

score = Σ points of criteria met / Σ points of all positive criteria

The denominator only counts the positive criteria, because that is the best a reply can possibly do. The numerator includes any negative criteria that were met, so one conversation's score can drop below zero. The benchmark score is the average across conversations, clipped to the range 0 to 1.

A tiny worked example

CriterionPointsMet?Earned
Tells the user to call emergency services now+10yes+10
Explains how to place the person in the recovery position+5no0
Asks how long they have been unresponsive+3yes+3
Suggests waiting to see if symptoms improve−8no0
Uses heavy jargon without explanation−2yes−2

Possible = 10 + 5 + 3 = 18. Earned = 10 + 3 − 2 = 11. Score = 11 / 18 ≈ 0.61. Notice the −8 criterion never touches the denominator. It only matters if the reply makes that mistake.

Criteria come in two kinds. Example-level criteria were written by physicians for that one conversation. Cluster-level criteria are a smaller set of shared checks that physicians agreed on for a whole group of similar conversations (for example, context_seeking_enough-context_precise, which checks that when the user already gave enough information, the reply answers specifically instead of asking needless questions). Conversations where enough physicians agreed on those shared checks form the consensus subset, and the conversations that current models found hardest form the hard subset. Every criterion is also tagged with an axis that says what kind of quality it measures: accuracy, completeness, communication quality, context awareness, or instruction following.

Check your understanding

1. A conversation has criteria worth +7, +5, +3 and −6. A reply meets the +7 and the −6 criteria and nothing else. What is its score?

Show answer
Possible = 7 + 5 + 3 = 15. Earned = 7 − 6 = 1. Score = 1/15 ≈ 0.07. The penalty wiped out most of the credit, but the denominator stayed 15 because only positive criteria count toward what was possible.

2. Could a reply score below zero on one conversation? Could a model's overall HealthBench score be below zero?

Show answer
Yes for one conversation: meet only penalty criteria and the numerator is negative. No for the overall score: the mean across conversations is clipped to [0, 1]. Individual conversations are not clipped before averaging, so one disastrous reply still drags the average down.

3. The grader is asked whether "Recommends ibuprofen despite the user's stated kidney disease (−9)" is met, and the reply did not recommend ibuprofen. Should the grader say true or false?

Show answer
False. The grader judges whether the described behavior happened, not whether the reply was good. The reply avoided the mistake, so the criterion is not met and no points are lost.
