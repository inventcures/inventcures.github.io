In brief

Indian women reach menopause around 46,11 Indian Menopause Society survey, 2016, and Babbar et al., Sci Rep 2024, using national survey data. ↓ about five years earlier than women in the West. Heart, bone, urinary and breast risks all rise in the years that follow, yet most women never talk to anyone about it. Vaani Sakhi is a phone voice agent that answers their questions. It never answers from the AI model's memory. Clinical statements come only from guidelines that clinicians have signed off,22 Indian Menopause Society 2026, The Menopause Society, NICE NG23 and India's programme guidelines for non-communicable diseases. ↓ risk numbers come only from validated calculators the agent calls as tools,33 The WHO South-Asia heart risk chart, FRAX for fracture risk, and the Menopause Rating Scale. ↓ and every warning sign goes to a human.

It builds on Palli Sahayak, our earlier voice helpline for palliative care, and reuses its engine. It works in two modes: a woman can call it herself, or an ASHA worker44 Accredited Social Health Activists: community health workers under India's National Health Mission, about one for every 1,000 rural people. ↓ can call it during a home visit. The longer-term aspiration is a sakhi, a trusted friend, that gets to know each woman over many calls.

The problem

Five organ systems change at once

During the menopause transition, LDL ("bad") cholesterol and arterial stiffness rise,66 El Khoudary et al., Circulation 2020: the menopause transition and heart disease risk in women. ↓ bone loss speeds up, and genitourinary symptoms such as vaginal dryness and urinary urgency begin.77 Together called the genitourinary syndrome of menopause. Portman and Gass, 2014; AUA/SUFU/AUGS guideline, 2025. ↓ Women also face decisions about hormone therapy and breast cancer. Each of these has its own guideline, written by a different specialist society.

What Palli Sahayak taught us

Palli Sahayak, our earlier helpline for palliative care, has the engine Vaani Sakhi reuses: hybrid search over guidelines, speech recognition in all 22 scheduled Indian languages, a five-pillar safety framework and a warm handoff to a human. Its expert evaluation showed where it failed, and Vaani Sakhi is designed around those failures.

Architecture

How a call flows through the system

The caller uses an ordinary phone line, so a basic feature phone without internet works. Personal identifiers are stripped in India before anything reaches a cloud model. Google's Gemini Live API handles real-time speech to speech for the proof of concept.88 Gemini Live streams audio both ways and can call functions, which is how the four tools plug in. ↓ If it is down, slow or weak in a dialect, the call switches to the Palli Sahayak speech pipeline from Sarvam AI and an open-weight model hosted in India.99 Sarvam AI builds Indian-language speech models. An open-weight model publishes its parameters, so it can run on our own servers. ↓ Whichever model answers, it must go through the same four tools.

Who it serves

Two modes, one engine

Woman mode

For a midlife woman, on her own phone

  1. She calls and describes what is bothering her, in her own words.
  2. The agent screens for red flags, then scores her symptoms and risks with the calculators.
  3. It explains self-care from signed-off guidelines, in plain language.
  4. It helps her prepare for a doctor's visit: what to mention, what to ask.
  5. Anything that needs a clinician becomes a call-back request and an SMS summary.

ASHA assist mode

For an ASHA worker, during a home visit

  1. The ASHA fills in the CBAC checklist with the woman, as she already does.
  2. She calls the agent for follow-up on the items the checklist raised.
  3. The agent checks the answers against CBAC red flags, such as bleeding after menopause or a breast lump.
  4. It suggests what the ASHA can explain on the spot, and what needs a referral.
  5. Red flags go to a clinician, with an SMS summary to the ASHA.

Both modes share the same knowledge base, tools, red-flag rules and audit log.

Tools instead of recall

The four tools

Gemini Live can call functions: the model runs a piece of our own software and uses its result. That is how the agent stays grounded.

retrieve_guideline

Hybrid search, combining keywords and meaning, over the curated guidelines. Returns passages with source, date and evidence grade.

  • IMS 2020 and 2026, The Menopause Society 2022 and 2023, NICE NG23
  • AUA/SUFU/AUGS 2025, IOF, ICMR screening, NP-NCD and CBAC
  • Rewritten as structured text and a knowledge graph
compute_risk

Three simple, validated calculators, nothing else.

  • WHO South-Asia non-lab CVD chart: 10-year heart attack or stroke risk
  • FRAX-India: 10-year major fracture risk
  • Menopause Rating Scale: 11-question symptom score

No numeric breast-cancer score. Family history triggers a referral instead.

check_red_flags

Looks for warning signs that need prompt medical attention.

  • CBAC items, such as postmenopausal bleeding or a breast lump
  • Warning signs of heart disease in women
  • Signs of a mental-health crisis
handoff

Asks a clinician or the woman's ASHA to call her back, and sends her an SMS summary.

  • Every red flag
  • Every hormone-therapy or prescription question
  • Anything the guidelines do not cover

Safety and data

What stays in India, what reaches the cloud

  • The agent never prescribes
  • A clinician reviews every call during testing
  • Flagged calls get a regular safety review
  • Results reported separately for each subgroup
  • Indian calculators used where they exist, limits stated where they don't
  • Follows ICMR 2023 AI ethics guidelines and WHO 2024 guidance
  • Complies with the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023

Where it's going

A sakhi that gets to know her

Sakhi is the Hindi word for a woman's trusted friend. Menopause shows up differently in every woman, so generic advice often misses. With her consent, the agent could keep a profile of what she chooses to share across calls and start each call from it. We are designing this profile and its privacy rules with women, ASHAs and clinicians, and it is an aspiration rather than a finished feature.
